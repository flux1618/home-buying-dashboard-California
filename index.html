Two ways this dataset misleads you, both measured rather than assumed.
A rating label is not a scale. FEMA bins its five labels separately by hazard. Compare the percentile, not the adjective; earthquake is the anchor tract’s 95th percentile exposure.
A blank is not a zero. The anchor tract has no wildfire rating, not a low wildfire score: it is dense Boyle Heights urban fabric with no wildland to model. Treat the blank as unrated, then use the parcel’s fire-zone and insurance quote. The same rule applies wherever FEMA does not publish a tract value for drought, heat wave, or landslide.——

    The five decisions you actually need to make · in order

    1. Confirm the top-2 submarkets by Nov 2026. Start with Boyle Heights/El Sereno for commute and price, then test Alhambra/Monterey Park for school outcomes and house fit. Drive at the actual weekday hospital-arrival window; walk candidate streets after dark.
    2. Lock the $190K down-payment target by Dec 2026. It is the profile’s 20% down assumption on the $950K target. Keep reserves outside the down payment; the dashboard does not model mortgage insurance below 20%.
    3. Get two insurance quotes before contract. Quote standard homeowners and earthquake coverage separately. A hillside or fire-zone address is a different insurance product from the Boyle Heights flatlands.
    4. Underwrite the buyer tax, not the seller tax. Pull the parcel’s TRA, model the Prop 13 reset, reserve for the supplemental bill, and file the homeowners’ exemption on time.
    5. Trigger buy or wait in Apr 2027 using the timing table. Buy signal: supply remains available and your target house clears the school/commute tests. Wait signal: a material LA WARN cluster plus a Port cargo reversal. Otherwise, execute the normal purchase.
    Discipline note. Protecting capital comes first. Your household has strong income and a specific, near-in-time window. The edge is not predicting price or rate — it is choosing a submarket that fits your commute + fiber + schools rules, and moving decisively when supply and time-on-market look this favorable.

    Run the full engine

    This page is a snapshot on purpose — static files, no backend, no keys, works offline. The analyzer behind it does the part a snapshot cannot: it goes and looks things up.
    What this page does
    • Scores facts you type in, against thresholds compiled from buyer_profile.toml
    • Applies deductions, capital-expense tiers, the unknown-facts cap, and the verdict bands in the same order the engine does
    • Models affordability, rent-vs-buy, and down-payment runway from the sliders
    What only the engine does
    • Geocodes the address, then pulls the county parcel record, FEMA flood layer, school context, and drive time to Keck Hospital of USC — so hard fails are looked up, not typed
    • Parcel-specific TRA property tax with the Prop 13 base-year reset on sale, supplemental-assessment flag, and homeowners’ exemption
    • Full amortisation, insurance proration, cash-to-close, and three separate maintenance-reserve methods reported side by side rather than averaged
    • Scores a whole CSV shortlist at once and ranks it
    • Says clearly when a source was unreachable instead of quietly guessing
    Three doors, one engine. A CLI, a batch runner, and an HTTP API all call the same scoring core — they cannot disagree about a house. This page is the fourth door, and it is the one that can't import Python, so its rules are compiled out of the profile by tools/build_snapshot.py and a CI check fails the build if the two ever drift apart again.
